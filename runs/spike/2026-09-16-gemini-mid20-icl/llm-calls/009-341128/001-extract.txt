2. `relations`: relationships STATED OR CLEARLY IMPLIED in the text between two extracted entities, as { "head", "type", "tail", "evidence" }.

   - `type`: a short lower-case verb phrase with hyphens (e.g. `attacks`, `uses`, `delivers`, `targets-sector`, `exploits`, `hosts`, `attributed-to`, `affects`, `located-in`, `part-of`, `sends-email-to`).

   - `evidence`: a short verbatim fragment of the text (at most 15 words) supporting the relation.
   - Do NOT invent relations the text does not support. Few or no relations is a correct answer. Do NOT add a relation for every co-occurring pair.
3. `newRelationTypes`: [{ "name", "definition" }] for relation types you introduced; an empty array is the normal case.

- Malware Family: A malicious software program, variant, or family designed to compromise systems, exfiltrate data, or disrupt computer operations. (also: Malware, Malicious Software)
- Software Vulnerability: A security flaw or weakness in software, hardware, or system architecture that can be exploited by threat actors. (also: Vulnerability, Security Flaw, CVE)

### KNOWN RELATION TYPES ###
- located-in: Specifies the geographical country or territory where an organization or entity is located or based.
- part-of: Indicates that an entity is a structural component, agency, or division of another entity.
- uses: Indicates that an organization or actor employs a specific tool, platform, standard, or contact mechanism.
- exploits: Indicates that an entity takes advantage of a specific security vulnerability.
- hosts: Indicates that network infrastructure or a domain stores and serves a file or service.
- delivers: Indicates that a file, program, or channel drops, downloads, or installs a payload.
